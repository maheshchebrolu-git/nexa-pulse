# Regulatory Compliance: CPUC & California TNC Rules

**Purpose.** This policy summarizes the core California and San Francisco regulatory obligations that bind Nexa Pulse operations and assigns ownership for compliance.

**CPUC authority.** Nexa Pulse operates under a Transportation Network Company permit issued by the California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC) — the TNC subclass of a TCP-P permit, a category created by CPUC Decision 13-09-045 — and must comply with the Public Utilities Code (sections 5351-5420) and the General Order 157 series. TNC permits expire after three years and must be renewed before lapse, since a lapse suspends operating authority.

**Core mandates.** Required at all times: driver background checks (national criminal and sex-offender databases plus DMV history), a 19-point vehicle inspection before service and annually or every 50,000 miles, whichever comes first, a driver training program, a zero-tolerance drug-and-alcohol policy, and the period-based insurance described in the Insurance & Liability Coverage Periods policy. All trips must be prearranged; street hails are prohibited. TNCs must contribute 0.33% of their gross California revenues into the CPUC Transportation Reimbursement Account (PUCTRA).

**Accessibility.** Compliance with SB 1376 / the Access for All program and the $0.10 per-trip Access fee, plus the accessibility-plan section of the CPUC annual report.

**Driver classification.** Under Proposition 22 (passed November 2020, upheld by the California Supreme Court in Castellanos v. State of California, July 2024), Nexa Pulse drivers are independent contractors provided the company does not set their hours, require acceptance of specific requests, or restrict working for other platforms. Operations practices — especially repositioning and incentives — must preserve this voluntariness. Proposition 22 also requires the company to provide qualifying drivers: a net earnings floor of 120% of the applicable minimum wage for engaged time plus roughly $0.30 per engaged mile (inflation-adjusted); a healthcare subsidy of 82% of the average Covered California premium for drivers averaging at least 25 engaged hours per week, and 41% for those averaging 15-25 hours; and occupational-accident insurance covering medical costs and lost income for on-the-job injuries.

**San Francisco taxes.** Trips originating in San Francisco are subject to the city's Traffic Congestion Mitigation Tax.

**Data reporting.** Annual trip-data reports to the CPUC, with public versions appropriately redacted.

**Ownership.** The compliance team owns filings and audits; operations managers must ensure day-to-day practices (inspections current, hours-of-service enforced, accessibility honored) remain compliant.
