# Operations Escalation Procedures

**Purpose.** This policy defines when and how a Nexa Pulse operations manager escalates beyond routine handling, and the chain of ownership for high-stakes situations in San Francisco.

**When to escalate.** Escalate immediately when a situation involves: a Tier 1 safety incident; a suspected data breach; a regulatory inquiry, citation, or potential permit issue; a price-gouging-cap decision during a declared emergency; media attention or potential litigation; a platform outage degrading matching or safety features; or any situation outside documented policy.

**Escalation tiers.**

- **Tier A — on-shift lead.** Operational anomalies: a zone in severe sustained under-supply, an incentive misfire, an SFO staging disruption. The shift lead can authorize incentive changes and repositioning beyond a manager's standing limit.
- **Tier B — duty manager / safety team.** Serious safety incidents, deactivation questions, regulatory contacts, significant refunds beyond a manager's authority.
- **Tier C — senior leadership / legal / compliance / communications.** Critical safety events, data breaches, regulatory enforcement, litigation, or media.

**Process.** The escalating manager opens an incident record, states severity and the decision needed, preserves relevant data (trip records, timestamps, communications), and hands off with a concise summary. Time-critical safety matters are escalated by phone, not queue.

**Authority and documentation.** Escalation does not pause emergency action: calling 911 and preserving safety always precede internal notification. Every escalation is logged with timestamps, decisions, and owners for post-incident review.

**Communications discipline.** Only authorized spokespeople communicate externally about escalated matters; operations managers route media and regulator contact to Tier C and do not respond directly.

**Review.** Tier B and Tier C escalations receive a post-incident review to identify policy gaps and update these documents.
