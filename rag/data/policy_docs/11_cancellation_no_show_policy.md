# Cancellation & No-Show Policy

**Purpose.** This policy defines when cancellation and no-show fees apply, the wait-time grace periods, and how disputes are handled, for trips in San Francisco.

**Trip status lifecycle.** A trip moves `requested` to `in_progress` (after pickup) to `completed`, or to `cancelled` at any point before pickup. This policy applies to trips that end in `cancelled`.

**Rider cancellation.** A rider may cancel without a fee within a short grace window after a driver accepts (for standard rides, roughly two minutes; longer for premium tiers). After the grace window, a cancellation fee applies if a driver has already made meaningful progress toward pickup. No fee is charged if the system detects the driver made no progress or is running materially late (five or more minutes behind the estimate).

**No-show.** If a driver arrives at the pickup location and the rider does not appear, the driver must wait through the wait-time grace period (standard tier: arrival timer counts down; the driver becomes eligible to claim a no-show after the posted window, commonly around five to seven minutes), attempt to contact the rider, and only then mark the rider a no-show to collect the fee. Skipping these steps voids fee eligibility.

**Driver cancellation.** Drivers may cancel without penalty for legitimate safety or policy reasons. Cancellations made to manipulate incentives or avoid short trips are monitored; excessive driver cancellation rates trigger review under the Driver Deactivation & Account Standards policy.

**Wait-time fees.** Separate from cancellation, per-minute wait-time fees may accrue after the grace period. Riders with a disability-related waiver are exempt from wait-time fees and are refunded prior charges on request.

**Exemptions.** Cancellations linked to a service-animal denial never incur a rider fee. The operations dashboard tracks zone-level cancellation rates as a marketplace-health signal — a rising cancellation rate alongside high `request_count` often indicates a supply shortfall, not rider behavior.
