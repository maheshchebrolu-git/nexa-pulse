# Tourist-Zone Operations (Marina / Fisherman's Wharf)

**Purpose.** This policy governs operations in the Marina / Fisherman's Wharf tourist zone, whose demand profile differs markedly from commercial and residential zones.

**Demand pattern.** Tourist demand peaks midday through evening and on weekends and holidays, is weather- and season-sensitive, and skews toward short hops to other attractions and to hotels in the Financial District/SOMA. Riders are often visitors unfamiliar with the area, which raises the rate of pickup confusion and miscommunication.

**Pickup challenges.** Crowded, congested curbs at the Wharf make driver-rider matching harder. Operations should promote clearly named pickup landmarks and encourage in-app communication. Mis-pickups and "can't find driver" cancellations are more common here; the cancellation policy and its no-progress/late-driver exemptions should be applied generously.

**Supply strategy.** Tourist demand is more predictable by calendar than by minute, so pre-planning beats reactive surge. Operations should forecast weekend and event-driven peaks and use modest pre-positioning incentives rather than allowing large surge spikes that frustrate visitors. Because tourist trips are often short and low-fare (toward the lower end of the $8-$45 range), incentive design should reward completed trips and good ratings rather than raw count.

**Service quality.** First-time riders disproportionately shape Nexa Pulse's reputation. Drivers serving the Wharf should be reminded of service basics (accurate vehicle metadata so visitors can identify the car, patience with navigation). Accessibility obligations apply fully in tourist areas.

**Cross-zone flow.** The dashboard should track outbound flow from the Marina/Wharf into hotel-dense zones so returning drivers can be redirected toward the next under-supplied zone rather than dead-heading back.
