# Late-Night & Nightlife Operations

**Purpose.** This policy governs Nexa Pulse operations during late-night hours (roughly 9 p.m. to 3 a.m.), concentrated in the Mission and Castro nightlife zones, with spillover in SOMA. It is distinct from Peak-Hours & Commute Operations, which addresses weekday daytime commuting.

**Demand pattern.** Nightlife demand is bursty and outbound: `request_count` in the Mission and Castro climbs sharply around bar and venue closing times, often outpacing the `available` driver pool. Demand is sensitive to weekends, holidays, and Pride and other Castro/Mission events.

**Supply strategy.** Because many drivers go `offline` late at night, operations should pre-position incentives into the Mission and Castro ahead of closing windows and monitor for the hours-of-service timeout wave that can thin supply exactly when demand peaks. Repositioning should favor short hops from quieter adjacent areas.

**Safety emphasis.** Late-night trips carry elevated safety risk: intoxicated riders, higher incident rates, and more lost items. Operations should staff the Incident Response and Lost and Found queues more heavily on weekend nights. Drivers should be reminded they may decline or end trips involving unsafe or abusive behavior, and may cancel for legitimate safety reasons without penalty.

**Pricing.** Surge engages under the Dynamic Surge Pricing Policy to balance the closing-time spike, capped at standard ceilings unless an emergency cap applies. Transparent upfront pricing is especially important for intoxicated riders.

**Pickup management.** In dense nightlife corridors, designated pickup points reduce curbside chaos and double-parking, an SFMTA concern. Operations may communicate pickup-point guidance during peak closing windows.

**Review.** Track post-closing ETAs, unfilled `requested` trips, and incident/lost-item volume to refine the next weekend's plan.
