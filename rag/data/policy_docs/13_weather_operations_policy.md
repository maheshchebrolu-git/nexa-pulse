# Weather Operations Policy

**Purpose.** This policy governs Nexa Pulse operations during adverse weather in San Francisco, where conditions are highly localized — coastal fog, winter atmospheric-river rain, and high winds.

**Weather and demand.** Rain and fog reliably raise `request_count` while reducing the number of `available` drivers, producing rapid under-supply. The dashboard ingests weather alerts and flags zones likely to spike. Inner Sunset and the western neighborhoods are especially affected by dense marine-layer fog; the Marina and Embarcadero corridor are exposed to wind.

**Supply support.** During sustained adverse weather, the operations manager should proactively deploy repositioning incentives ahead of the demand spike rather than after, and may allow surge to engage to keep supply on the road. Driver-facing communications should remind drivers to reduce speed, increase following distance, and use headlights in fog.

**SFO interaction.** Summer marine-layer fog can compress SFO arrival capacity and shift arrival banks by 30-90 minutes, creating delayed, clustered airport demand. Coordinate with the SFO Airport Pickup & Staging Procedures during fog events.

**Price-gouging guardrail.** Ordinary bad weather is not an emergency. However, if the Governor, the President, or a local authority declares a state of emergency (for example, a major storm), California Penal Code Section 396 prohibits excessive and unjustified price increases on essential goods and services — generally capping increases at 10% above the pre-emergency price for 30 days (and 180 days for repair/reconstruction services), renewable by further proclamation. In a declared emergency, surge multipliers in affected zones must be capped accordingly and the operations manager must switch those zones to emergency-capped pricing. When in doubt, cap rather than surge.

**Safety suspension.** If conditions become genuinely unsafe (flooding, downed power lines, road closures), operations may advise drivers to go `offline` in affected areas and should never incentivize travel into hazardous zones.

**Review.** After significant weather events, operations records demand, supply, ETA, and any capped-pricing decisions for compliance and planning.
