# Peak-Hours & Commute Operations

**Purpose.** This policy governs weekday commute operations (roughly 6-9 a.m. and 4-7 p.m.), centered on the Financial District and SOMA. It is deliberately distinct from Late-Night & Nightlife Operations: commute demand is directional, recurring, and tied to transit hubs and offices rather than venues and bars.

**Demand pattern.** Morning peaks bring inbound demand toward the Financial District and SOMA and around transit hubs (Caltrain, BART, the Transbay Terminal); evening peaks reverse the flow outbound toward residential zones (Inner Sunset, Castro, West Portal) and the Marina. `request_count` in the commercial core is sharply time-boxed and highly predictable day-to-day.

**Supply strategy.** Because the pattern repeats, operations should pre-position supply before each peak rather than chasing it. Morning: stage `available` drivers toward the Financial District/SOMA approaches. Evening: anticipate the outbound wave and seed drivers who can carry riders into residential zones, then redirect them to the next peak rather than dead-heading back. Repositioning incentives should be timed to the leading edge of each peak and wound down as the ratio normalizes.

**Congestion management.** The commercial core is congested and curb-constrained during peaks. Drivers must avoid double-parking, bike lanes, and transit lanes; promoting designated pickup points near office clusters reduces friction and complaints.

**Pricing.** Routine surge handles commute imbalances within standard ceilings. Because commuters are price-sensitive and repeat users, prefer pre-positioning to large multipliers where possible.

**Hours-of-service interaction.** Drivers who work both the morning and evening peaks risk hitting the 10-hour driving cap mid-evening; the dashboard should forecast this and plan fresh supply for the evening peak.

**Review.** Track peak ETAs, unfilled requests by zone, and the morning/evening directional balance to refine the next day's pre-positioning.
